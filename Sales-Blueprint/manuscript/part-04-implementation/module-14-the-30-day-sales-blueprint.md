# Module 14: The 30-Day Sales Blueprint

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- Install the nine-stage Blueprint Framework into your business over four structured weeks, rather than all at once
- Sequence the rollout so each week's habit has something solid to build on
- Know specifically what should be different in your pipeline by day thirty
- Use the framework's worksheets, in order, as the actual mechanism of installation, not just as reading material

## Everything so far, condensed

Every module in this book has followed Kestrel Office Interiors and Priya Shah through one framework, one stage at a time. What the book hasn't shown you directly is how Priya actually installed all of it in her own business in the first place, before Bramble Analytics ever called. She didn't read the framework and become fluent in all nine stages overnight. She rolled it out over a month, one or two stages at a time, testing each one on live conversations before adding the next. This module is that rollout, written so you can run the same one.

Trying to adopt all nine stages simultaneously, on every deal, in the first week, is the most common way this framework fails in practice. Sequencing it, the way the four weeks below do, is what actually makes it stick.

## Week 1: Build Trust and Learn the Customer

Spend this week on the two stages every later one depends on. Complete Worksheet 3 (trust audit) before your next first meeting with a new prospect, and identify, in advance, which of the three trust signals (competence, reliability, candour) that specific buyer is most likely to need. In every existing conversation, ask at least one of Module 4's three discovery questions you haven't been asking: who else needs to be comfortable with this, what the buyer is personally measured on, or what would be hard to defend internally. By the end of the week, you should be able to name the full buying committee, not just your main contact, on every live deal.

## Week 2: Understand the Problem and Establish Value

Take the two or three deals furthest along in your pipeline and run Worksheet 5 (problem diagnosis) on each: separate the stated symptom from the actual cause, and quantify the cost in the buyer's own numbers rather than yours. Once the diagnosis is confirmed with the buyer, build a value stack for each deal using Worksheet 6, with at least three distinct, itemised outcomes rather than one broad benefit. By the end of the week, every live deal should have a confirmed diagnosis and a value stack the buyer has already agreed with, not just a product description.

## Week 3: Present the Offer, Remove Doubt, and Inspire Action

Rebuild your proposal template around Worksheet 7: one recommended package, an explicit scope boundary, and a specific, dated next step, rather than a menu of equal options. As doubts surface on live deals, use Worksheet 8 to ask what's specifically behind each hesitation rather than reassuring generally. And for any deal where the buyer has agreed in principle but hasn't acted, apply Worksheet 9: diagnose the friction, shrink the next step, and attach a genuine deadline if one exists. By the end of the week, you should have at least one deal move from agreement to a signed commitment using this exact sequence.

## Week 4: Nurture Relationships, Turn Customers into Advocates, and Systems

In the final week, look back at every customer you've closed in the last year and pick three you haven't spoken to since delivery. Use Worksheet 10 to plan a genuine, no-pitch check-in for each. For the one you're most confident is satisfied, use Worksheet 11 to make a specific referral ask rather than a vague one. Finally, rebuild your pipeline using Worksheet 13, tagging every open deal to its actual Blueprint stage, and choose the small number of metrics (win rate, referral rate, time to signature) you'll track from this point on.

## What should be different by day thirty

You won't have closed every deal in your pipeline using the full framework in one month; most sales cycles run longer than that. What you should have, by day thirty, is a pipeline where every open deal is correctly tagged to a real stage, a proposal template that leads with a recommended package instead of a menu, at least one resolved doubt and one shrunk next step you can point to directly, three genuine nurture conversations completed with past customers, and one specific referral ask made rather than hoped for. Those are the concrete, checkable signs that the system, not just the reading, is now running.

## Common mistakes

- Trying to apply all nine stages to every deal simultaneously in week one, rather than installing them in sequence
- Treating the four weeks as a reading schedule instead of a doing schedule tied to worksheets and live deals
- Skipping Week 4's systems work because it feels less urgent than the deals in front of you right now
- Measuring success by how much of the book you've read rather than what's changed in your actual pipeline

## Chapter summary

- The nine-stage framework installs best in sequence, two or three stages at a time over four weeks, not all at once.
- Each week's habit is tied to a specific worksheet and applied to live deals, not read about in the abstract.
- By day thirty, the concrete signs of progress are a correctly tagged pipeline, a rebuilt proposal template, at least one resolved doubt, completed nurture conversations, and a specific referral ask, not a finished deal count.
- The system Priya built this way is the same one that later won Bramble Analytics, Tom's remote deal, and everything that followed it.

## Key takeaway

**You don't need to master nine stages at once; you need thirty days to install them one week at a time.**
