# How to Use This Book

## Read Part One and Part Two in order

Part One explains why most sales advice fails and how buyers actually make decisions. Part Two is the Blueprint Framework itself, nine chapters, one per stage, in the order a real deal moves through them. Each chapter builds on the last, so read Modules 3 to 11 in sequence the first time through, even if you're tempted to skip to the one that matches your current problem.

After that first read, use the book as a reference. If you're stuck on pricing objections next month, go straight to Module 8. If you've just closed a deal and need to write the follow-up email, Module 10 has the templates.

## Follow Kestrel Office Interiors

Every chapter in Part Two continues the story of Kestrel Office Interiors, a fictional office fit-out company run by a founder named Priya Shah. You'll meet her in Module 1, watch her struggle with the exact problem each chapter addresses, and see what changes once she applies that chapter's stage of the framework. Her business is fictional, but her problems are not: they were built from the same patterns that show up in real founder-led sales conversations, again and again.

Watch what stays constant about Kestrel (her business, her market, her team) and what improves (her win rate, her pipeline, her referrals). The improvement is the point. It shows you the framework working over time, not just in a single conversation.

## Do the exercises with your own business

Every chapter includes a worksheet or exercise in the `/worksheets` folder, referenced by filename at the point you need it. These are built to run on your actual business, your actual prospect, your actual price. Reading about the value stack is not the same as building one for what you sell. Twenty minutes with a worksheet, applied to a real deal you're working on right now, will teach you more than the chapter alone.

## Use the templates as starting points, not scripts

The `/templates` folder holds reusable documents (proposal structures, follow-up emails, persona builders) that Part Two introduces and Part Three (Modules 12 and 13) helps you turn into a repeatable system. Adapt the wording to how you actually talk. A template that reads like a corporate memo will lose the trust you're trying to build.

## The five questions

Every chapter in Part Two answers the same five questions, in the same order: what the concept is, why it works, how to do it, what to try in the next twenty-four hours, and how to know if it worked. If you only have ten minutes with a chapter, read the "How do I apply this today?" and "How do I measure success?" sections first. They tell you exactly what to do and how to check that it's working.

## Module 14 turns the book into a plan

Once you've read Parts One through Three, Module 14 compresses the whole framework into a thirty-day rollout: what to install in your business each week, in what order, with what you should expect to see change by day thirty. That's the chapter to return to once you've finished the book and want to actually build the system, rather than just understand it.
