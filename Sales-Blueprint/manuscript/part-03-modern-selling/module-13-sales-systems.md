# Module 13: Sales Systems

## Learning objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- Turn an individually-run framework into a system that tracks every deal without relying on memory
- Track each deal, and each stakeholder within it, by its actual Blueprint stage rather than a generic pipeline label
- Build a small set of dashboard metrics that tell you whether the system is actually working
- Manage the framework's loop-back, when a new stakeholder or a referral restarts an earlier stage, without losing track of where everyone else stands

## Why this module exists

Tom's remote deal from Module 12 didn't arrive alone. The published write-up that found him also produced two more inbound enquiries within a fortnight, on top of the ongoing nurture cadence with Bramble Analytics and a handful of older clients. Priya's old system for tracking all of this was a notebook and her memory, which had worked when she had one or two live conversations at a time. It stopped working the week she nearly missed following up with one of the new leads because she'd been focused entirely on Tom.

A framework that only works when one person holds every deal in their head isn't a system; it's a habit that breaks under load. This module is about the difference.

## Track deals by Blueprint stage, not generic labels

Most off-the-shelf pipeline tools use generic stages: lead, qualified, proposal, closed. Those labels don't tell you what to do next, because they don't map to a specific action. Priya rebuilt her pipeline board around the nine Blueprint letters instead: every open deal sits under Build Trust, Learn the Customer, Understand the Problem, and so on, through to Turn Customers into Advocates. A deal sitting under R (Remove Doubt) tells her exactly what needs to happen next: find and resolve the specific doubt, using Module 8's approach, not a vague instruction to "follow up."

## Track people, not just deals

The framework's loop-back, covered briefly in the overview chapter, means a single deal can have different stakeholders at different stages simultaneously: Priya might be well into Present the Offer with Dominic while a newly-involved finance contact on a different deal is still at Build Trust. A system built only around deals loses this distinction. Priya's board tracks each stakeholder within a deal separately when more than one is involved, so a new name joining midway through a decision doesn't get silently assumed to be at the same stage as everyone else.

## The dashboard: a small number of honest metrics

A system is only useful if it tells you whether it's working. Priya tracks four numbers, reviewed monthly: win rate (deals closed against deals quoted), average time from first contact to signature, referral rate (the share of new deals arriving through an introduction rather than cold outreach), and the split between in-person and remote-sourced deals. None of these numbers exist to look impressive; each one answers a specific question about whether the framework is being applied consistently.

> **Figure 13.2: The Sales Dashboard.** See `visuals/charts/figure-13.2-sales-dashboard.mmd`. Win rate and referral rate, tracked monthly, are the two numbers that most directly reflect whether the framework's earlier stages are being run well.

Since adopting the framework, Priya's own numbers have moved in one direction: win rate up from under one in four quotes to something closer to one in two, and a growing share of new deals arriving through referral rather than cold outreach, exactly the pattern the framework predicts when trust, diagnosis, and nurture are all being run deliberately rather than left to chance.

## Apply this today

Rebuild your own pipeline, however simple, around the nine Blueprint letters instead of generic stage labels. For each open deal, write down which letter it's actually at and the one specific action that letter calls for, using the relevant module's guidance.

## Measure success

You'll know this is working when you can look at your pipeline and immediately know the next action for every open deal, without having to reconstruct the history from memory. Rising win rate and referral rate, tracked over several months rather than a single deal, are the concrete signs the system is compounding rather than just organising.

## Common mistakes

- Using generic pipeline labels that describe progress without specifying the next action
- Tracking deals but not the individual stakeholders within them, and losing track when a new person joins midway
- Measuring vanity numbers instead of the few metrics that actually reflect whether earlier stages are being run well
- Letting the system exist only in one person's head, which breaks the moment volume increases

## Chapter summary

- A framework that depends entirely on one person's memory is a habit, not a system, and it breaks under load.
- Tracking deals by their actual Blueprint stage tells you the next specific action; generic pipeline labels don't.
- Stakeholders, not just deals, need to be tracked individually, because the framework loops back for each new person separately.
- A small number of honest metrics, reviewed regularly, tell you whether the system is actually working.

## Key takeaway

**A system is a framework that survives having more than one deal on your desk at once.**
