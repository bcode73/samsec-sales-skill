# Glossary

Terms as used throughout this book, in alphabetical order.

**Advocate.** A past customer who proactively refers, reviews, or defends the business without being asked each time. See Module 11.

**Budget holder.** The person who controls or releases the money for a purchase. Not always the same person as the decision-maker; see Module 2.

**Buying committee.** The small, often informal group of people who together decide on a considered purchase, even when no one in the organisation calls it a committee. See Module 2.

**Candour signal.** A trust signal built from admitting a real limitation before the buyer discovers it themselves. See Module 3.

**Champion.** A person inside the buying organisation who wants you to win and will advocate internally, even when not in the room. See Module 7.

**Competence signal.** A trust signal built from specific, checkable proof that you can do the exact job in front of you. See Module 3.

**Considered purchase.** A purchase the buyer will not make on impulse: it involves risk, cost, or more than one decision-maker.

**Decline.** A genuine no, distinct from an objection, which is a signal of unresolved doubt rather than a final answer. See Module 8.

**Economic buyer.** The person whose approval is ultimately required because they own the budget outcome.

**Objection.** A signal of unresolved doubt, not a rejection. See Module 8.

**Reliability signal.** A trust signal built from proof you do what you say, when you say it. See Module 3.

**The Blueprint Framework.** The book's central nine-stage system for winning and keeping a customer: Build Trust, Learn the Customer, Understand the Problem, Establish Value, Present the Offer, Remove Doubt, Inspire Action, Nurture Relationships, Turn Customers into Advocates. See the Blueprint Framework chapter in the front matter.

**The 30-Day Sales Blueprint.** The book's closing implementation plan: a four-week rollout of the full framework for a real business. See Module 14.

**Trust deficit.** The gap between how much confidence a buyer needs to say yes and how much they currently have. See Module 3.

**Value stack.** The visible, itemised list of outcomes a customer gets, arranged so the price feels small next to the pile. See Module 6.
