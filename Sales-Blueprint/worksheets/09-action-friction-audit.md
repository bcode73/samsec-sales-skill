# Worksheet 9: Action Friction Audit

Use this on a deal where the buyer has agreed in principle but hasn't yet acted.

## Step 1: Confirm it's friction, not doubt

Has the buyer stated a specific concern (a doubt, see Worksheet 8), or have they said something close to "yes, we want to do this" with no action following? If it's an unstated concern, go back to Worksheet 8 first.

## Step 2: Ask plainly what's in the way

Call or message the buyer directly: "What's stopping this from moving forward?" Record their answer.

## Step 3: Diagnose the friction

- [ ] Competing priorities: this task hasn't reached the top of their list
- [ ] Unclear process: they're not sure who needs to sign or approve
- [ ] Effort mismatch: the remaining step feels bigger than the decision itself

## Step 4: Shrink the next action

Rewrite the outstanding task as the smallest, most specific, scheduled action you can offer. Not "review and sign," but a bounded call, a single page, a specific time.

## Step 5: Name a genuine deadline, if one exists

Is there a real constraint (a schedule slot, a resource availability, a seasonal factor) that gives this a true deadline? Write it down. Do not invent one if none exists.

## Step 6: Apply it forward

Deliver the shrunk next step and the genuine deadline together. Note how long it takes from this point to a committed action, and compare it to how long the deal had already been stalled.
