# Worksheet 3: Trust Audit

Use this before your next first meeting with a new prospect.

## Step 1: Ask about the last attempt

What is the one question you'll ask to find out what happened the last time this buyer tried to solve this problem? Write it out in full, in the words you'll actually say.

## Step 2: Predict the deficit

Based on what you already know about this buyer, guess which trust signal is likely to matter most to them:

- [ ] Competence: they need proof you can actually do this
- [ ] Reliability: they need proof you'll do what you say, on time
- [ ] Candour: they need proof you'll tell them the truth, including bad news

Write one specific, checkable example you can offer for each signal, ready to use if the conversation calls for it:

## Step 4: Choose your candour signal in advance

Naming a limitation on the spot is harder than it sounds. Decide now, before the meeting, which true limitation of your product or service you will say out loud, unprompted, in the first conversation.

## Step 5: Apply it forward

After the meeting, note whether the buyer volunteered any information they wouldn't tell a stranger (a budget figure, an internal disagreement, a past bad experience). If they did, your trust-building worked. If they didn't, revisit Step 2: you likely led with the wrong signal.
