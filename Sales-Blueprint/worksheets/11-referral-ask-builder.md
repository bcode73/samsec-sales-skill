# Worksheet 11: Referral Ask Builder

Use this with a customer who has recently confirmed a good result, even in passing conversation.

## Step 1: Confirm the moment

Has this customer seen tangible proof of the result, not just a promise? If not, wait until they have; that's the moment this stage works best.

## Step 2: Name a specific person or type of contact

Who, specifically, would you like an introduction to? A named person is stronger than a general category, but a specific category ("another operations lead at a company your size") still beats a vague request.

## Step 3: Write the specific ask

Draft the exact question you'll ask, aimed at one bounded action (an introduction, a specific conversation), not a general request to keep you in mind.

## Step 4: Plan the loop-closing message

Write, in advance, the short note you'll send this customer after the introduction happens, regardless of whether it leads anywhere. Reporting back, win or lose, is what makes the next ask easier.

## Step 5: Apply it forward

Make the ask. Log the outcome and the date you closed the loop. Over time, track how many introductions convert to new customers, and how many of those new customers eventually become advocates themselves.

## Step 6: If no specific person comes to mind

If Step 2 comes up empty, write an alternative, equally specific ask instead: permission for a named case study, a short testimonial about the specific problem solved, or a public review. Use this version rather than defaulting to a vague, general request.

## Step 7: Check your own asking frequency

Before you ask this customer again in future, check: is this ask driven by something they mentioned unprompted, or by your own need for a new lead? If it's the latter, wait for a genuine opening instead.
