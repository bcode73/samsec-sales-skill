**2026-06-27**: Research baseline — MoR vs payment-processor distinction, provider fees, and tax-coverage comparison captured from live sources on this date (created alongside the sales-paddle platform skill). Re-verify fees/coverage before relying — they change often. Key facts: a **Merchant of Record** (Paddle, Lemon Squeezy, Polar, Creem, Gumroad) is the legal seller and **remits** global sales-tax/VAT/GST + takes liability; a **processor** (Stripe/PayPal) only moves money — with Stripe you stay the seller of record and **Stripe Tax calculates but does not remit**. Headline fees (best-effort): Paddle ~5%+$0.50, Lemon Squeezy ~5%+$0.50, Polar ~10%+$0.50+processing, Creem lower (~3.x%), Stripe ~2.9%+$0.30 (not MoR). Stripe's **international effective rate** climbs toward ~5% (intl card +1.5%, FX +1%, Stripe Tax +0.5%), narrowing the gap before counting compliance labor. Tax coverage depth: **Paddle most mature** (US states, EU VAT, UK, AU GST, parts of Asia); Lemon Squeezy good with CA-provincial/India-GST edge cases; Polar inherits Stripe Tax (US+EU). **Lemon Squeezy has no purchase orders / net terms** — Paddle (or Stripe Invoicing) for B2B/enterprise. Switching providers forces payment-method **re-tokenization** (often a customer re-subscribe) + subscription migration + tax-registration transition — costly, do rarely. Lemon Squeezy is Stripe-owned (2024). Resolved the backlog "Merchant of Record strategy" problem space.
